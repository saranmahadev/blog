<Sidenote label="True story">This is how it really went. I have left out dates and numbers because I would rather say less than guess.</Sidenote>

I did not start with Drafted. I started on Medium.

I will say one thing plainly: I was not consistent. I will not blame the platform for that. The inconsistency was mine.

What I can say is that the limits I ran into there were the platform's, not mine.

## The Four Limits

There were four of them, and they were all versions of the same thing: someone else was deciding.

- **Who saw a post** depended on the platform's algorithm and distribution, not on the people who chose to follow me.
- **How a post could look** was limited. I could not shape the format or the design the way I wanted.
- **My audience and my links** lived on the platform's terms. The address, the followers and the archive were all in someone else's hands.
- **How a post could earn** was the platform's call, through its monetisation and paywall rules.

None of that is a scandal. It is simply what it means to write on a platform that you do not own. But it was enough to make me ask where my writing should actually live.

## The Same Four, on My Own Site

<Compare>
  <Side kind="before" label="On a platform">The algorithm decides who sees it. The format is theirs. The audience and links live on their terms. Their rules decide how it earns.</Side>
  <Side label="On Drafted">You follow by feed, with no algorithm in between. I decide the format, down to the type. The pages are plain files on my own domain. There is no paywall and there are no ads.</Side>
</Compare>

Each line on the right is something I built on purpose. The earlier posts in this series explain how: no images and a design made of type, a blog that is just files, feeds done properly, no tracking.

## Why This Became Part of Dev Universe

Dev Universe is the system around my work, and every part of it has a job. For Drafted, the job is deep thinking and written knowledge: the place where an idea is developed in full sentences, in one place that I control.

YouTube and Instagram are still part of Dev Universe, and they should be. They do different jobs, for different people. Writing was the part where I wanted to own the page it lived on.

<Statement>I wanted to own the place my ideas **live**.</Statement>

If you want the story of how the site itself was made, it starts in [Where Drafted began](/building-drafted/where-drafted-began/). The rest of this series walks through every other part of Dev Universe, one at a time.

**Platforms are good places to be heard. A site of your own is where your work stays yours.**
